# Chroma Awards Season 2: rules for a game entry

Read on 2026-11-04 from the official rules (https://chromaawards.com/terms) and the Puzzle & Strategy
category page (https://chromaawards.com/prizes/game-puzzle-strategy). The organiser may change the rules;
re-read both pages before submitting.

## Dates

| Item | Date |
|---|---|
| Submission period | 2026-10-19 12:00 PT to **2027-01-07 23:45 PT** |
| Judging | 2027-01-07 to 2027-02-10 |
| Winners announced | about 2027-02-11 |
| Gala | about April 2027 |

A submission enters preliminary judging **as soon as it is submitted**. After preliminary judging it
**may not be changed**. Drafts can be saved before submitting.

## Judging criteria (equal weight, each scored 1 to 10)

| Criterion | Weight | What judges look for |
|---|---|---|
| Theme & Division Purpose | 25% | Follows the rules, fits the category, satisfies the division's goal (for games: gameplay) |
| Creativity & AI Innovation | 25% | Originality in world-building and design, and meaningful, non-gimmicky use of AI |
| Craft & Technical Polish | 25% | Production value, clean visuals, no technical glitches |
| Sound & Emotional Impact | 25% | Emotional resonance: character depth, audio mixing, dialogue, score |

Rounds: preliminary pass/fail plus a 1-10 score by up to 2 judges; top 66% per category advance;
quarter finals (top 50); semi finals (top 22); finals by up to 4 judges. Sponsor tool awards are judged
separately by each sponsor's judges, from the tool tag and proof of use.

## Category fit: Game Division, Puzzle & Strategy ($2,000 / $1,000 / $500)

Thematic guidelines on the category page:

- **Clever systems**: logic, planning and strategy built into mechanics that are easy to learn and
  satisfying to master.
- **Clear rules**: goals and feedback understandable **without long tutorials**.
- **Craft & technical execution**: game feel, mechanics, art direction, audio, overall polish.

Other game categories: Arcade & Party, Experimental & Open Format, Interactive Narrative, Music & Rhythm,
Meshy Game of the Year. A project enters exactly one category.

## Hard requirements

- **At least one AI tool** must be used to make the submission. Tool use may have to be disclosed; it must be
  transparent and non-deceptive.
- **Playable on the web without a download**, through a public web link (not unlisted, private or
  password protected) that can be **embedded in the project gallery**.
- In-game text and dialogue in **English**, or with English subtitles.
- A **download link to a gameplay video** and a **thumbnail**.
- Original work only: code, art, music and likenesses the entrant has rights to; no third-party IP or
  franchises; music must be original, licensed, permitted, or generated by AI.
- Must play as depicted on its intended platform.
- Entrant 18 or over, not in a sanctioned country, entering as an individual or team (not a company).
- Sponsor tool awards (e.g. ElevenLabs, Meshy, SpriteCook): tag the tool, use it significantly, give proof
  (explanation, screenshot or estimated percentage). These prizes stack with category prizes.

## What this means for the game

- The judges play remotely and score four equal criteria. Today the project is a simulation with no
  screens, no audio and no AI tool in it, so three of the four criteria have nothing to score yet.
- "Without long tutorials" and short judging sessions argue for an entry point that shows the economy's
  depth in minutes, not an open sandbox that pays off over years of game time.
- The link must work **embedded in the gallery** (an iframe on another site). Multithreaded WebAssembly
  needs cross-origin isolation (COOP/COEP headers), which an embedded frame generally does not get
  unless the parent page is isolated too. The turn must therefore meet its budget single-threaded, or
  fall back to it cleanly.
- Because a submission is frozen after preliminary judging, the judged build should live at a fixed,
  versioned URL, while development keeps deploying elsewhere.
